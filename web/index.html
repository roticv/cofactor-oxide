cofox cofactor-oxide

Integer factorization written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly. Everything runs locally in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

A decimal number or an expression such as 2^127-1 or (2^61-1)(2^89-1), up to 512 bits. Press Enter to factor.

How it works

Small factors are stripped cheaply first, and each remaining piece is either proven prime or handed to the next, more powerful method. Not every stage exists yet; when a composite can't be split, it's shown with a dashed outline.

  • Trial divisiondone Removes every factor below 4096.
  • Pollard rhodone Brent's variant with Montgomery arithmetic. Finds factors up to about 12 digits.
  • Baillie–PSW primality testdone Decides when to stop. No composite is known to pass it.
  • Pollard p−1 and Williams p+1planned Primes p where p−1 or p+1 has only small factors.
  • Elliptic curve methodplanned Factors up to 30–40 digits, whatever the size of the number.
  • Quadratic sieve, then number field sieveplanned General-purpose methods for hard composites.

WebAssembly lacks a 64×64→128-bit multiply, so the browser build runs about 3× slower than the native cofox command-line tool.
